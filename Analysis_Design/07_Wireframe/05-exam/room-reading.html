The history of glass

A  From our earliest origins, people have used glass. Natural glass, formed when lightning strikes sand or volcanic rock cools rapidly, was shaped into tools long before anyone learned to make it.

B  The first manufactured glass probably appeared as a glaze on pottery. Over centuries, craftsmen discovered that adding lime and soda made the material more durable, and glass vessels became objects of trade across the region. Note

C  It was the invention of the blowpipe, around the first century BC, that transformed the industry. Glassblowers could now produce thin, transparent containers quickly and cheaply, and for the first time glass was within reach of ordinary households.

D  In medieval Europe, the most celebrated use of glass was in religious buildings, where coloured windows told stories to congregations who could not read. Production remained a closely guarded skill, passed from master to apprentice.

E  Today, glass is engineered for purposes its early makers could never have imagined, from fibre-optic cables that carry data across oceans to screens that respond to the lightest touch.
